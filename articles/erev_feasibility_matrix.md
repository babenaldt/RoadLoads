## Legend

- ✅ **0-1%**: Generator can handle this alone - start at any SOC
- ⚡ **2-50%**: Battery assist needed - percentage shows minimum starting SOC
- ❌ **N/A**: Cannot complete - power demand exceeds combined generator + battery capacity

## Key Insights

### For Pro-EREV Argument:
1. Base vehicles handle ALL cycles with no battery assist
2. Light-duty towing (Flatbed Utility) works perfectly on generator alone
3. Even challenging climbs (Davis Dam, I-70) are completable with moderate battery assist
4. The generator-sustained capability provides true range anxiety elimination

### For Anti-EREV Argument:
1. 6 of 14 configurations CANNOT complete Highway 75 MPH at all
2. Heavy towing requires 20-50% battery assist even on shorter cycles
3. The 100 kW generator (Scout) struggles with most trailer configurations
4. Even the 130 kW generator (Ram) fails at sustained highway speeds with heavy trailers

## The Uncomfortable Truth

The data shows EREVs are excellent for:
- Daily driving without towing (use as pure EV)
- Light towing on varied terrain (flatbed, small utility trailers)
- Emergency range extension (generator can get you to a gas station)

The data shows EREVs struggle with:
- Heavy towing at sustained highway speeds
- High-drag trailers (RVs, livestock trailers, moving trailers)
- Any scenario where average power demand exceeds generator capacity

**Bottom line:** An EREV is the right choice if your typical use case is commuting with occasional light towing. It's the wrong choice if you regularly tow heavy loads at highway speeds.
